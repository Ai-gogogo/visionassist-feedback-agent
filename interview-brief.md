Study title: How should VisionAssist warn and answer?

Who: members of our VisionAssist testers panel who opt in after the survey. AI-moderated, about 8 minutes, text or voice.

Context to read first (plain words): VisionAssist is a student research prototype, not a mobility aid. Two phone cameras and a laptop detect obstacles, speak alerts such as "Caution, chair straight ahead, about 1 meter", and a local AI answers questions like "What is in front of me?".

Goals:
1. When should an alert be spoken while walking, and what words make it actionable?
2. What makes people trust or distrust an automatic warning?
3. How long and in what order should spoken answers be?

Guide (adapt follow-ups to each answer, one question at a time):
1. Think of a time you tried the demo or watched the video. What did you notice first about the alerts?
2. Imagine walking at your normal pace toward a chair. When would you want to hear about it, and what exactly should it say?
3. What would make you stop trusting the warnings? What would win your trust back?
4. When you ask "What is in front of me?", what should the answer say first, and what can it leave out?
5. If you could change one thing before our final demo, what would it be?

Close by thanking them and reminding them answers are only used for our class project.
